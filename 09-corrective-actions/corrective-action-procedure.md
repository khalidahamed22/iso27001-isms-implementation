## 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes a consistent process for identifying, recording, investigating and correcting information security nonconformities.

It ensures that the organisation addresses the causes of nonconformities, implements appropriate corrective actions and evaluates whether those actions are effective.

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to nonconformities identified through:

- Internal audits.
- External audits.
- Information security incidents.
- Security control assessments.
- Vulnerability assessments.
- Supplier security reviews.
- Management reviews.
- Employee reports.
- Monitoring and operational activities.
- Customer or regulatory feedback.

## 6. Corrective Action Process

### Step 1 – Identification

A nonconformity may be identified through an audit, incident, assessment, monitoring activity or other relevant process.

The person identifying the issue must record:

- Date identified.
- Description of the issue.
- Source of identification.
- Relevant requirement.
- Available objective evidence.
- Affected process or control.

### Step 2 – Immediate Correction and Containment

The responsible process owner shall evaluate whether immediate action is required to correct the issue or limit its consequences.

Examples include:

- Revoking unauthorised access.
- Applying an urgent security patch.
- Correcting an inaccurate asset record.
- Restricting access to exposed information.
- Restoring a missing security configuration.

Immediate correction does not automatically eliminate the underlying cause.

### Step 3 – Impact Evaluation

The responsible owner shall evaluate:

- Consequences of the nonconformity.
- Affected information assets.
- Potential confidentiality, integrity and availability impacts.
- Legal and contractual implications.
- Whether similar issues exist elsewhere.
- Whether the information security risk register requires updating.

### Step 4 – Root Cause Analysis

The process owner shall investigate why the nonconformity occurred.

Suitable methods include:

**5 Whys Analysis**

Repeatedly ask why the issue occurred until the underlying process or control weakness is identified.

**Fishbone Analysis**

Examine possible causes across categories such as:

- People.
- Processes.
- Technology.
- Management.
- Resources.
- External factors.

Root cause conclusions must be supported by evidence rather than assumptions.

### Step 5 – Corrective Action Planning

The responsible owner shall define actions that address the identified root cause.

Each action should include:

- Corrective action description.
- Responsible owner.
- Required resources.
- Target completion date.
- Expected outcome.
- Verification method.

Actions should be proportionate to the consequences of the nonconformity.

### Step 6 – Implementation

Assigned owners shall implement approved corrective actions.

Implementation evidence may include:

- Updated policies and procedures.
- Configuration records.
- Access control records.
- Security testing results.
- Training records.
- Change management records.
- Updated risk assessments.

The corrective action register shall be updated to reflect progress.

### Step 7 – Effectiveness Verification

An authorised reviewer shall verify whether:

1. The corrective action was implemented.
2. The identified root cause was addressed.
3. The issue has not recurred within an appropriate observation period.
4. Relevant controls are operating as intended.
5. Associated risks and documentation have been updated where necessary.

The person verifying effectiveness should be sufficiently independent of the implementation work to provide an objective assessment.

If the action is ineffective, further investigation and corrective action are required.

### Step 8 – Closure

A corrective action may be closed only when:

- Implementation evidence is available.
- Effectiveness has been evaluated.
- Required records have been updated.
- Closure has been authorised by the designated reviewer.

An action must not be closed merely because its target date has passed or an owner reports that the work is complete.

## 7. Corrective Action Statuses

| Status | Description |
|---|---|
| Open | Nonconformity recorded |
| Under Investigation | Root cause analysis in progress |
| Action Planned | Corrective action approved |
| In Progress | Corrective action being implemented |
| Pending Verification | Implementation completed; effectiveness review outstanding |
| Closed | Effectiveness verified and closure approved |
| Reopened | Previously closed issue found ineffective or recurring |

## 8. Escalation

The Information Security Manager shall escalate:

- Significant or repeated nonconformities.
- Overdue high-priority corrective actions.
- Issues involving legal or contractual obligations.
- Corrective actions requiring additional management resources.
- Actions found ineffective during verification.

## 9. Records and Retention

The organisation shall maintain appropriate documented information, including:

- Nonconformity records.
- Root cause analysis.
- Corrective action plans.
- Implementation evidence.
- Effectiveness verification records.
- Closure approvals.

Records shall be protected against unauthorised access, alteration and loss.

Retention periods shall follow the organisation's approved records retention requirements and applicable obligations.

## 10. Monitoring and Reporting

The Information Security Manager shall periodically monitor:

- Number of open nonconformities.
- Corrective actions overdue.
- Average corrective action completion time.
- Repeated nonconformities.
- Actions awaiting effectiveness verification.
- Corrective action effectiveness.

Significant trends shall be reported to senior management during management reviews.
