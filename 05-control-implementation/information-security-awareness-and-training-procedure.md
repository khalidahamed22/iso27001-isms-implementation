# Information Security Awareness and Training Procedure

**Document ID:** PROC-009  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Organisation:** CloudLedger Technologies (Pvt) Ltd  
**Status:** Draft – Simulated Portfolio Document  
**Classification:** Internal  
**Related ISO Requirements:** Clause 7.2, Clause 7.3, Annex A 6.3

## 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes how CloudLedger Technologies identifies information security awareness and training needs, delivers relevant training, evaluates employee understanding and maintains training records.

The objective is to ensure that personnel understand their information security responsibilities and contribute to the protection of organisational information and systems.

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to:

- Permanent and temporary employees.
- Developers and IT personnel.
- Contractors and consultants.
- Personnel with privileged system access.
- Other individuals who perform work under the organisation's control, where relevant.

Third-party personnel must receive appropriate security awareness information according to contractual requirements and their level of access.

## 3. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Executive Management | Provide support and resources for awareness activities |
| Information Security Manager | Define awareness requirements and oversee the programme |
| HR Department | Coordinate onboarding and maintain employee training records |
| Department Managers | Identify role-specific training needs and monitor completion |
| IT and Security Teams | Deliver technical security awareness and guidance |
| Employees | Participate in required training and follow security policies |

## 4. Awareness and Training Requirements

The organisation must identify training needs based on job responsibilities, information access, security risks and relevant changes.

| Training Area | Target Audience | Suggested Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Information Security Policy | All personnel | At onboarding and annually |
| Phishing and Social Engineering | All personnel | At onboarding and periodically |
| Password and Authentication Security | All personnel | At onboarding and annually |
| Data Classification and Handling | All personnel handling organisational information | At onboarding and when requirements change |
| Incident Reporting | All personnel | At onboarding and annually |
| Secure Software Development | Developers and technical personnel | At onboarding and periodically |
| Privileged Access Security | Administrators and privileged users | At onboarding and annually |
| Cloud Security Awareness | Cloud administrators and relevant technical teams | At onboarding and when responsibilities change |
| Supplier and Third-Party Security | Procurement and supplier owners | Role-based and periodically |

The frequencies above are proposed organisational targets, not fixed ISO 27001 requirements.

## 5. New Employee Awareness

HR must coordinate information security awareness during employee onboarding.

The onboarding process should include:

1. Provide the employee with the Information Security Policy.
2. Explain acceptable use and access responsibilities.
3. Explain information classification and handling requirements.
4. Explain how to report suspected security incidents.
5. Provide phishing and social engineering awareness.
6. Deliver additional role-specific training where necessary.
7. Record training completion and acknowledgement.

Employees must complete mandatory onboarding awareness within the timeframe established by management.

## 6. Ongoing Awareness Activities

The Information Security Manager should coordinate ongoing awareness activities, including:

- Security awareness sessions.
- Phishing awareness campaigns.
- Security newsletters and internal announcements.
- Policy updates and reminders.
- Practical security scenarios and exercises.
- Lessons learned from security incidents.
- Targeted awareness for personnel with specialised responsibilities.

Awareness activities should reflect current organisational risks and emerging threats.

## 7. Training Effectiveness Evaluation

Training effectiveness should be evaluated using appropriate methods, such as:

- Knowledge assessments and quizzes.
- Training completion rates.
- Employee feedback.
- Phishing simulation results, where authorised.
- Observed compliance with security procedures.
- Relevant incident trends and recurring security mistakes.

Completion alone does not establish that training was effective. The organisation should use evaluation results to identify additional training needs.

## 8. Training Records

HR and the Information Security Manager must ensure that relevant records are maintained.

Records may include:

- Employee training attendance.
- Training completion certificates.
- Assessment results.
- Policy acknowledgements.
- Awareness campaign records.
- Phishing simulation results.
- Training effectiveness reviews.
- Identified training gaps and corrective actions.

Access to training records must be restricted to authorised personnel.

## 9. Non-Completion and Corrective Actions

Where personnel fail to complete mandatory training:

1. HR or the responsible manager sends a reminder.
2. The employee is given an appropriate completion deadline.
3. Outstanding completion is escalated to the relevant manager.
4. Repeated non-completion is escalated to the Information Security Manager and HR.
5. Additional training or corrective action is assigned where necessary.

## 10. Monitoring and Performance Indicators

The Information Security Manager should monitor:

| Indicator | Measurement |
|---|---|
| Training Completion Rate | Percentage of assigned personnel completing mandatory training |
| Onboarding Training Completion | Percentage of new employees completing required awareness |
| Assessment Pass Rate | Percentage meeting the defined assessment threshold |
| Phishing Simulation Results | Click, reporting and submission rates, where simulations are conducted |
| Outstanding Training | Number of overdue training assignments |

Targets must be established and approved by management.

## 11. Review

This procedure must be reviewed annually or when significant changes occur in organisational risks, technology, legal requirements or employee responsibilities.
